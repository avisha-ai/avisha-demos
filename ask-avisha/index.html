Overall heat transfer coefficient

1/U = 1/ho + Rf,o + xw/kw + Rf,i + 1/hi

Thin-wall form, all terms on one area. For thick tubes on the outside area, multiply the inside terms (Rf,i, 1/hi) by do/di and use do·ln(do/di)/(2kw) for the wall. §10.1

What fouling does

Fouling adds the resistances Rf,o and Rf,i, so U falls. At the same area and temperature difference, the heat duty Q = U·A·ΔTlm falls in the same proportion. §10.2

U = 678 W/m²·K · 73% of clean (clean U ≈ 930, with the sample data below)

What baffles do

Closer baffle spacing raises the shell-side velocity, so ho goes up, and the shell-side pressure drop goes up too. Spacing is a trade-off fixed at design. Baffles also support the tubes against vibration. §2.2, §10.3

Sample text written for this demo. Values marked sample value are illustrative, not real specifications. Always follow your own site procedures and the real maker's manual.

1. What HX-101 is

HX-101 is a shell-and-tube heat exchanger. One fluid flows through the tubes (tube side); the other flows around the tubes inside the shell (shell side). Heat passes through the tube walls. The two fluids never mix. The fluids flow in opposite directions (countercurrent), which moves the most heat for this size of exchanger.

2. Main parts

2.1 Tube bundle: the tubes, fixed in a tube sheet at each end. 2.2 Baffles: plates across the shell that hold the tubes in place and stop them vibrating, and make the shell-side fluid flow back and forth across the tubes instead of straight along the shell. Crossflow moves heat better. Baffles also cause much of the shell-side pressure drop. 2.3 Vents and drains: a vent at the high point and a drain at the low point on each side. 2.4 Relief device: protects the shell from overpressure.

3. Before start-up

3.1 Permit or sign-off in place; know which fluid is on which side. 3.2 Bolts, gaskets, insulation and guards in place; no visible leaks. 3.3 Drains closed, vents ready, valves in the start-up position, relief devices in service and not isolated. 3.4 Pressure gauges and thermometers on both sides fitted and readable.

4. Start-up

4.1 Bring the cold side in first. Open the inlet slowly and vent air from the high point until only liquid comes out. Trapped air blankets tube surface, so less heat is transferred, and can cause hammering. 4.2 Then bring the hot side in slowly and vent it the same way. Heating up slowly avoids thermal shock and stress at the tube joints and gaskets. 4.3 When warm, check again for leaks and record pressure drops and temperatures as the baseline.

5. Normal running

5.1 Each shift, record inlet and outlet temperatures and the pressure drop on both sides. 5.2 Compare with the clean baseline at the same flow. Sample baseline at design flow and normal inlet temperatures: tube-side pressure drop sample value 0.40 bar; cold-side outlet temperature sample value 60 °C. 5.3 A rising pressure drop at the same flow, or outlet temperatures drifting away from the baseline, means the exchanger needs attention (see sections 6 and 9). 5.4 Sample normal range: pressure drop up to sample value 20% above baseline, cold-side outlet no more than sample value 3 °C below baseline. Outside this range, the reading is unusual: check it and trend it.

6. Fouling

6.1 Fouling is a layer of deposit (scale, sludge, corrosion products, biological growth) on the tube surfaces. 6.2 Signs: less heat transferred (the cold outlet is cooler and the hot outlet is hotter than the baseline at the same flows), and often a slowly rising pressure drop. 6.3 Trend the readings; one reading is not enough. Plan cleaning when the trend reaches the limit set by your site. Sample cleaning trigger: sample value: pressure drop 1.5 × clean baseline.

7. Shutdown and cleaning

7.1 Shutdown: reduce and isolate the hot side first, then the cold side, so the exchanger cools without overheating the cold side. 7.2 Never open, clean or remove covers while HX-101 is running or under pressure. Cleaning needs a shutdown, both sides isolated, depressurised, drained, cooled and made safe under your site's permit. 7.3 Tubes can be cleaned mechanically or chemically by trained people following the cleaning procedure.

8. Safety

8.1 Do not exceed the design pressure or temperature shown on the nameplate. 8.2 Never isolate the relief device while the exchanger is in service. 8.3 Wear the protective equipment your site requires; surfaces can be hot.

9. Troubleshooting

9.1 Pressure drop rising: fouling or blockage in the tubes or shell; a blocked upstream strainer; a partly closed valve; or a flow higher than normal. Check the flow and valves first, then trend for fouling. 9.2 Not enough heating or cooling: fouling; air not vented; a flow lower than normal; or an inlet temperature different from normal. 9.3 Leak or one fluid found in the other: report it and follow your site procedure; a tube or gasket may be leaking. Isolate as your procedure says. 9.4 Strange noise or vibration: knocking or hammering can mean trapped air (venting is described in 4.1); rattling or vibration can mean a flow higher than normal or loose tubes or baffles (2.2). Check the flow first. If the noise is new or getting worse, report it and follow your site procedure.

10. Design data (for engineers)

10.1 Overall heat transfer coefficient, thin-wall form with all terms on one area: 1/U = 1/hₒ + R_f,o + x_w/k_w + R_f,i + 1/hᵢ. For thick tubes on the outside area, the inside terms are multiplied by dₒ/dᵢ and the wall term becomes dₒ·ln(dₒ/dᵢ)/(2k_w). 10.2 Fouling adds the resistances R_f,o and R_f,i, so U falls; at the same area and temperature difference the heat duty Q = U·A·ΔT_lm falls in the same proportion. 10.3 Closer baffle spacing raises shell-side velocity, so hₒ rises, and the shell-side pressure drop rises too; spacing is a trade-off fixed at design. 10.4 Sample design data: hₒ sample value 1,500 W/m²·K, hᵢ sample value 3,000 W/m²·K, stainless tube wall sample value 1.2 mm, k_w 16 W/m·K, fouling allowance sample value 0.0002 m²·K/W each side.
